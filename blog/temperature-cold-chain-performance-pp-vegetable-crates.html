Temperature and Cold-Chain Performance of PP Vegetable Crates

Vegetablecrates Editorial • September 2026

Cold-Chain Demands on Produce Packaging

Fresh vegetable distribution relies on unbroken cold chain to maintain shelf life, prevent microbial growth, and preserve nutritional quality. From field harvest in 90°F heat to refrigerated distribution center at 34°F, produce packaging must perform across a 60-degree temperature swing without compromising structural integrity or contaminating product.

Polypropylene corrugated crates are increasingly specified for vegetable cold-chain applications because they combine the ventilation needed for respiration with the moisture resistance and thermal stability that cardboard cannot provide. This guide evaluates PP crate performance in produce cold-chain environments, including temperature range, moisture management, and compliance requirements for food-contact surfaces.

Thermal Properties of Polypropylene in Produce Applications

Polypropylene exhibits a glass transition temperature of approximately -20°C (-4°F) and a melting point of 167°C (333°F). This thermal window covers the entire range of produce cold-chain conditions, from field heat to deep-chill storage. PP retains impact resistance and structural rigidity at temperatures as low as -10°F, making it suitable for frozen or super-chilled applications where polycarbonate or ABS alternatives become brittle.

In practice, vegetable crates and produce packaging from Elipacko are rated for continuous service from -22°F to 180°F. This range accommodates rapid hydrocooling (32°F to 34°F water spray for field heat removal), mechanical cooling (34°F to 38°F in refrigerated trucks), and controlled-atmosphere storage (32°F to 36°F with modified atmosphere packaging). The crates do not become brittle or crack under impact when dropped at 28°F—a common failure mode for polystyrene foam containers.

Thermal conductivity of twin-wall PP corrugated sheet is approximately 0.15 W/m·K, which provides insulation comparable to cardboard while allowing faster thermal equilibration in forced-air cooling systems. The fluted channel design creates air gaps that reduce conductive heat transfer and permit convective airflow through ventilation slots, accelerating cooling rates for dense produce loads like broccoli, lettuce, and sweet corn.

Moisture Resistance and Hygiene in Wet Produce Environments

Cardboard containers fail in wet produce applications—berries misted for freshness, leafy greens with residual field moisture, and root vegetables with soil loads that transfer moisture to container surfaces. Saturated cardboard loses compressive strength, collapses under stacking loads, and becomes a growth medium for mold and bacteria. PP corrugated crates are inherently hydrophobic and resist moisture absorption even during extended contact with wet produce.

Under normal use conditions, PP vegetable crates can withstand 72 hours of continuous moisture exposure without measurable strength loss. Unlike wax-coated cardboard, which delaminates after repeated wet-dry cycles, PP maintains its laminate bond and structural properties across hundreds of trips in wet produce service. This durability is critical for closed-loop distribution networks where crates are returned, cleaned, and reused within 7 to 14 days.

Hygiene protocols for PP produce crates are well-established. Washing with potable water and food-grade sanitizer removes soil and microbial contamination between trips. Unlike wood bins, which can harbor pathogens in surface cracks and require pressure washing or chemical sterilization, PP corrugated surfaces are smooth and non-porous, enabling effective cleaning with standard wash-rinse-sanitize procedures validated under FDA food safety programs.

Ventilation Design for Produce Respiration and Cooling

Fresh produce continues to respire after harvest, consuming oxygen and producing carbon dioxide, water vapor, and heat. Packaging for respiring commodities—leafy greens, broccoli, peppers, berries—must provide sufficient ventilation to remove heat and gas byproducts while maintaining appropriate relative humidity around the product.

PP corrugated crates are available with custom ventilation slot patterns optimized for specific produce types. Small-slot ventilation (3mm to 5mm openings) provides adequate airflow for dense loads like berry clamshells or bagged salad mixes, while larger slots (10mm to 15mm) are specified for bulk leafy greens and head lettuce where convective cooling is critical. The slot pattern is die-cut during manufacturing, ensuring consistent airflow across the fleet and predictable cooling performance.

For mixed-load distribution—where different produce types with varying respiration rates share a pallet—crate ventilation must balance competing requirements. Elipacko's engineering team provides custom ventilation design for PP corrugated vegetable boxes, modeling airflow resistance and cooling curves to ensure each layer of the pallet reaches target temperature within the required time window, typically 4 to 6 hours from field to cooler.

FDA Food-Contact Compliance for Produce Containers

PP corrugated crates used in direct contact with fresh produce are food-contact surfaces under FDA jurisdiction. The resin must be FDA-compliant for food contact, meaning it is manufactured from materials that have been reviewed and approved for use in contact with food under the conditions of intended use. Virgin polypropylene homopolymer and random copolymer grades are FDA-approved for food contact without extraction limitations in most applications.

Elipacko specifies FDA-compliant virgin PP resin for all produce packaging applications, with documentation available for regulatory submissions and facility food safety plans. The resin complies with FDA 21 CFR 177.1520 (olefin polymers) and meets the specific migration limits for food simulants relevant to fresh produce, including water, acid, and oil-based foods. This documentation supports SQF, BRC, and FSSC 22000 food safety certification for produce packers and distributors.

Supply Chain Continuity and Seasonal Demand Planning

Produce packaging procurement must align with seasonal demand curves—peak volume in spring and fall for leafy greens, summer for stone fruit and berries, and year-round for root vegetables and imports. PP corrugated crates, while more durable than cardboard, represent a capital investment that must be managed across seasonal cycles when crate utilization drops by 30% to 50% from peak.

Effective crate fleet management models inventory requirements based on peak-week shipping volumes, average trip time (including cleaning and return), and safety stock for equipment failure or unexpected demand spikes. Most produce operations maintain a fleet 20% larger than average demand to absorb peak-week surges and equipment downtime without disrupting shipments.

Elipacko's US-available inventory program allows produce packers to stage crates at regional distribution centers for rapid replenishment during peak season, reducing the capital tied up in off-season fleet storage. Custom sizing ensures crate dimensions optimize pallet patterns for specific produce types, maximizing truck utilization and reducing freight cost per pound of product shipped.
